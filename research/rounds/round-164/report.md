R163's union query costs are label-specific weighted coverage functions; fixed opening costs are also submodular. This is an instance of established optimization, not a novel mathematical problem.

Exact expansion to target label j from current assignment: client move node gain is new minus old frame saving; require new facility and newly activated query nodes (negative cost); refund old opening or query activation only if every corresponding old client moves (positive refund node implies ALL its move nodes). Maximum closure solves the exact best move. Literal is also an available target. Verify direct objective difference and 40 random small brute-force expansions. Three sweeps, no global optimality.

Global upper bound for gain: relax assignment exclusivity with nonnegative integer client prices p_i. For every feasible solution sum p_i plus sum over facilities max(0, max_X [sum_(i in X)(w_ij-p_i)-activation_j*|union Q_i|-opening_j]) bounds gain from above. Each inner maximum solved exactly with R163 min-cut (nonpositive adjusted weights can be omitted). Five price passes, retain minimum valid integer bound and independent per-client maximum benefit bound. No convergence or tightness promise. Verify on random exhaustive assignments.

Counterexample: k disjoint facilities each serve two clients of weight 10 with opening cost 19, one distractor serves one of each pair with weight 1 and cost k-2. Greedy takes gain 2 vs feasible gain k, then all other openings unprofitable. Thus no uniform positive approximation ratio for greedy NET SAVINGS as k grows (not an approximation claim about total cost). Verify k=12 with HiGHS MILP.
